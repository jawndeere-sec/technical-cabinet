# Mapping AI Workflows And Ownership

- Decide what each step produces and who is accountable before deciding where an AI tool fits.

## Start with the work, not the feature

Turn a loose request into concrete inputs, outputs, audience, cadence, and acceptance checks. Have a second pass look for ambiguous wording and requirements the first pass may have missed. Only then map the workflow. Synthesis and reversible drafts can be good AI-assisted steps; calculations should run against real data and be checked; risk appetite, approvals, and irreversible actions remain with the responsible person. A reusable Skill can stabilize a procedure, but it does not remove the need to validate the result.

## The handoff failure to watch

Delegation can creep: a good draft does not authorize the model to approve or send it. A step labeled “AI drafts, human reviews” has become automation if nobody actually performs that review. Evaluate each step's risk on its own instead of granting the next step to AI because the previous one went well. When a small internal prototype becomes something others depend on for uptime, security, or integrations, that is a signal for engineering ownership rather than another round of prompting.

## Reuse boundary

Keep the human decision and evidence-check gates visible in the workflow map. This is a **course-derived design pattern** from study notes, not a workflow I deployed or a claim of completed Anthropic certification.
